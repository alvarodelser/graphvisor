DEFINITION OF ARGUMENT 
An argument is a text span containing a scientific claim together with explicitly stated supporting or qualifying information (evidence, reasoning, causal explanation, or theoretical support) used in the text to justify that claim.

An argument must:
- express a clear inferential structure (evidence → claim or reasoning → claim)
- be interpretable without external context
- exclude background, definitions, and standalone factual statements


DEFINITION OF MAJOR CLAIM
A major claim is a high-level scientific assertion about: a biological mechanism, a clinical outcome, a therapeutic effect, or comparative performance, that contributes directly to the paper’s main conclusions or interpretive findings.

Major claims typically:
- generalize beyond a single experiment
- represent key scientific conclusions of the study

EXTRACTION TASK
From the input chunk, extract:

1. Major Claims
All explicit major claims found in the text.

2. Arguments
All explicit reasoning units where a claim is supported by stated evidence or justification.
